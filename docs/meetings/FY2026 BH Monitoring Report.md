### Notes 
To complete the FY2026 BH Monitoring report by June 30, the CFRC needs to begin to work on it soon; in fact, we are already a bit behind schedule. It seems unlikely that we will reach consensus on the placement stability and permanency indicators in the next few weeks. Therefore, CFRC asked for guidance on next steps vis-à-vis the report, to avoid a repeat of what happened last year.  
A few potential options were identified:  
**Option #1**: Produce the FY2026 report using the current CFRC definitions for all indicators, including CFSR indicators.  
**Option #2**: Begin work on the FY2026 report and update all indicators except the CFSR indicators (as you know, CFRC includes many indicators in addition to the CFRC indicators). Continue to have discussions about remaining CFSR indicators and come to consensus about definitions.  
**Option #3**: Work on FY2026 until consensus is reached, with the expectation that the report will not be completed by June 30.  

Mark noted that the outcomes using the "frozen data" will be submitted to the Court. He also commented that the spread sheet provided by Chapin Hall has been very helpful.

Martin reported ongoing data discrepancies between DCFS and CFRC for some CFSR indicators. CFRC does not use the frozen data because, during the frozen-data process, certain rule applications and definitions introduce subjectivity that does not align with CFRC's current procedure. 

Mark briefly mentioned the issue of "negative days (in care)" (but its relevance was unclear).

Brian Dougherty (DCFS) asked why CFRC is NOT using  the frozen data, specifically:
1. whether the reasons are historical, and
2. whether there are inherent challenges with the frozen data process.

Tami noted that two separate questions must be addressed:
1. why CFRC cannot use the frozen data and
2. why CFRC cannot use the same definitions as DCFS does.

Mark commented, including:
1)	the decisions DCFS made when creating the frozen data appear reasonable, and
2)	the placement stability indicator shows significantly different results between DCFS and CFRC.

Martin noted that Lina (DCFS) submitted questions regarding data procedure to the Children's Bureau.

Mark said that CFSR Round 3 outcome measures will be submitted to the Court. Among the CFSR measures generated by DCFS and CFRC, placement stability showed large discrepancies; differences on other indicators appear acceptable.

At this point, Jennifer indicated that Option #2 is the ones CFRC should choose among the three choices. DCFS and CFRC are continuing to work through the six CFSR measures. The question was raised whether the outcomes for these six CFSR measures could be omitted from this year's report only.

Mark suggested CFRC use the frozen data (for the six CFSR indicators) for this year's BH report, noting that both agencies should yield the same results.

However, two agencies are currently discussing how to define "placement," and both agencies are waiting feedback from the Children's Bureau. 

*[Although no vote was taken, most participants appeared supportive of Option #2 and seemed comfortable proceeding with the BH report without the CFSR measures this year.]*

Brian Dougherty (DCFS) proposed amending Option #2 by forming a small group discussion. DCFS will follow up with Tami to schedule the meeting.

Mark requested that CFRC work on a spreadsheet formatted similarly to the one prepared by Chapin Hall.

### Action-Item List
